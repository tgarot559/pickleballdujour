How to start pickleball

In shortTo start, you need a light paddle (7 to 8 oz, about 200 to 230 g), balls suited to indoor or outdoor play, and court shoes. Learn three rules first: underhand serve, two bounces, no volleying in the kitchen. Then play doubles: it's the most common format and the easiest to start with.

Starter gear

  • A light paddle. Around 7 to 8 oz (200 to 230 g) is easier on the arm and forgiving. Expect €45 to €85 for a good first paddle. Details in the paddle guide.
  • The right balls. Outdoor balls are heavier and harder to handle wind. Indoor balls are lighter and give more control. They have 26 to 40 holes depending on the model.
  • Court shoes. Not running shoes: their soles aren't built for side-to-side movement, and ankle sprains come fast.

What to wear

Regular sportswear is fine: shorts or skirt, breathable top. Nothing is required for casual play. What really matters is shoes. Outdoors, add a cap and sunglasses if you face the sun.

Doubles, singles or three players

Doubles is the main format: less court to cover and a social game. Start here.

Singles uses the same rules and court, but each player covers the full width. Much more physical, and the score has only two numbers, since there's one server per side.

Three players isn't an official format, but works well with friends: two against one rotating after each lost rally, or "kitchen only" games where you just dink in the two non-volley zones.

Five beginner habits

  1. Serve deep and safe. A serve that lands beats a hard one. You only get one try.
  2. Return deep, then move up. After your return, move to the kitchen line: that's where points are won.
  3. Stop hitting hard. Pickleball rewards patience. The low, slow ball into the opponent's kitchen (the dink) is your best friend.
  4. Keep your paddle up in front. Net exchanges are fast. Paddle low, and you'll always be late.
  5. Talk to your partner. "Mine", "yours", "leave it": half of doubles errors come from confusion down the middle.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start?

About €50 to €100: a paddle at €45 to €85 plus a set of balls. Many clubs lend gear for the first sessions.

Do I need to have played tennis?

No. Tennis, padel or table tennis players progress fast, but you can start from scratch.

How do you play pickleball with 3 people?

Casually: two against one rotating after each lost rally, or a dink-only game in the kitchen. It isn't an official format.

What shoes should I wear?

Court shoes (tennis, padel or indoor depending on the surface) with good ankle support for lateral movement. Avoid running shoes.
