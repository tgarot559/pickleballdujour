What is pickleball?

In shortPickleball is a paddle sport invented in the United States in 1965. It's played with a solid paddle and a perforated plastic ball on a badminton-sized court (44 × 20 ft, or 13.41 × 6.10 m), usually as doubles. Games go to 11 points, and only the serving team can score.

The easiest way to picture it: a mix of tennis, badminton and table tennis. From tennis it keeps the net and baseline rallies. From badminton, the court size. From table tennis, the solid paddle and the soft-touch game near the net.

What makes it unique is a 7 ft (2.13 m) zone on each side of the net called the kitchen. You can't volley the ball while standing in it. So nobody can camp at the net and smash everything. Points are built patiently, often with soft shots called dinks.

Where does pickleball come from?

It was invented in the summer of 1965 on Bainbridge Island, near Seattle, by Joel Pritchard, Bill Bell and Barney McCallum, who wanted a game for their families. Two stories explain the name: the Pritchards' dog, Pickles, who chased the ball, and the rowing "pickle boat", crewed with leftover rowers. Nobody has ever settled it.

Why everyone is picking it up

  • You can play within an hour. Underhand serve, a slower ball than tennis, a small court.
  • You keep improving for years. Behind the simplicity is real strategy: doubles positioning, the third shot, patience at the kitchen.
  • All ages play together. A teenager can rally with their grandparents, which is rare in racket sports.
  • It's cheap. A decent paddle starts around €45, and four pickleball courts fit on one tennis court.

Pickleball in numbers

The United States counted 24.3 million players in 2025 (SFIA). Canada had about 1.8 million in 2026. In France, where the French Tennis Federation (FFT) took official charge of the sport in early 2026, there are now more than 50,000 players and over 1,000 tennis clubs offering it, up from roughly 1,000 players in early 2024. Frequently asked questions

Is pickleball easier than tennis?

To start, yes: underhand serve, slower ball, smaller court. To play well, no: doubles strategy and kitchen play take years.

Why is it called pickleball?

Two stories: the Pritchard family dog, Pickles, or the rowing "pickle boat". Neither has ever been proven.

How many people play pickleball?

There is no reliable world figure. The US had 24.3 million players in 2025, Canada about 1.8 million in 2026 and France over 50,000 in late 2026.

Can you play pickleball with 3 people?

Not in official matches, which are singles or doubles. Casually, play two against one and rotate after each lost rally.
