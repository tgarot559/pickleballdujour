Tactics sheets for intermediate and advanced players

The third shot: drop, drive, or both

The drop remains the reference third shot: a slow ball that falls into the opposite kitchen forces the net team to hit up and lets you move forward. But at intermediate and advanced levels, dropping every time makes you predictable.

The drive has its place when the return is high or short: a low shot under the net tape, often followed by a drop on the fifth shot ("drive and drop"). Key cue: low, deep return, drop it; high or short return, drive it.

The reset from the transition zone

Mid-court is not a place to stay, but you always pass through it. When a fast ball comes at your feet, the goal is not to win the point: it is to put the ball back in the opposite kitchen and get back to a neutral rally.

Split step as your opponent strikes, paddle in front of your body with the face slightly open, and let the ball die on the paddle. No swing, firm wrist. Key cue: move up after a good reset, never during it.

Hand battles at the net

When all four players are at the kitchen and someone speeds up, the exchange often lasts under a second. The winner is the player whose paddle is already ready, up, in front of the chest.

Short motions, continental grip, no big backswing. Blocking towards the opponent's feet beats an ambitious counter that floats. Key cue: a ball coming above your shoulder often goes out; let it pass.

When to speed up: picking the ball to attack

Speeding up a low ball means hitting upwards, which hands your opponent a counter. The right ball to attack is above the net, in front of you, ideally after an opponent's dink that popped up.

Aim at the opponent's paddle-side hip or shoulder, or their feet: the hardest areas to defend. Key cue: if you have to bend down to hit it, it's a dink, not an attack.

Stacking in doubles

Stacking means both partners start on the same side when serving or returning, then split sides right after. The goal: keep each player's forehand in the middle, or the stronger player on the side that matters. It is legal as long as the correct player serves and the correct player returns, according to the score.

Start by stacking on serve, which is simpler, then on return, where the partner has to cross quickly after the return. Key cue: call the positions before every point ("stay", "switch").

The Erne and the around-the-post shot

The Erne means going around the kitchen to volley the ball level with the post, outside the sideline. It is legal as long as your feet do not touch the kitchen when you volley, momentum included. It is the answer to dinks hit too long down the line.

The around-the-post shot (ATP) is played on a very wide ball that pulls you off the court: the ball can go around the post instead of over the net, as long as it lands in the opponent's court. Key cue: practise these shots in training before trying them in a match.

Frequently asked questions

What level is this coach for?

It is designed for players who know the rules and can already sustain a rally: roughly 3.0 and up on DUPR. If you're just starting, begin with the guide to getting started.

Is the diagnostic free? Are my answers stored?

Yes, it's free with no sign-up. Everything is computed in your browser: your answers are neither sent nor kept.

How much time per week does it take?

Two sessions, each with about twenty minutes of drills, then open play. Two drills done well and scored beat ten done without counting.

Is driving the third shot a mistake?

No. The drop is still the foundation, but a low drive on a high or short return, followed by a drop on the next shot, is a weapon for intermediate and advanced players.
