Pickleball vs padel: the real differences

In shortPadel is played on an enclosed court with glass and mesh walls (20 × 10 m), with a pressurised tennis-type ball and tennis scoring. Pickleball is played on a smaller open court (13.41 × 6.10 m) with a perforated plastic ball, games to 11, and no volleying in the kitchen.
PickleballPadel
Court44 × 20 ft (13.41 × 6.10 m), open20 × 10 m, enclosed (glass and mesh)
WallsNoYes, the ball is played off the walls
PaddleSolid, no holes, about 16 × 8 inSolid foam with holes, thicker
BallHard perforated plastic (26 to 40 holes)Pressurised, close to a tennis ball
ServeUnderhand, diagonal, past the kitchenUnderhand after a bounce, diagonal
ScoringTo 11, only the serving team scoresTennis scoring: 15, 30, 40, games, sets
Key ruleNo volleys in the kitchen, two-bounce rulePlaying off the glass
FormatMostly doubles, singles possibleAlmost always doubles
Court costLow: four courts on one tennis courtHigh: glass structure

Which is easier?

Both are quick to learn. Pickleball needs less movement thanks to the small court, and the slower ball gives time to react. Padel's walls take a few sessions to master. For older or family players, pickleball is often more accessible.

Which is more physical?

Usually padel: bigger court, longer rallies off the walls, smashes. Pickleball is still a real workout, especially singles, but leans more on reflexes and touch than endurance.

Can you play both?

Of course. Many padel players pick up pickleball as a complement: net play and reading opponents carry over well. Just watch the instinct to volley close to the net, which costs dearly in pickleball because of the kitchen.

Frequently asked questions

What's the main difference between pickleball and padel?

Padel is played inside walls with a pressurised ball. Pickleball is played on a smaller open court with a perforated plastic ball and a non-volley zone.

Which is easier for beginners?

Pickleball, for most people: small court, slow ball, underhand serve.

Can I use a padel racket for pickleball?

No. A padel racket has holes and is thicker, and it doesn't meet pickleball rules.
