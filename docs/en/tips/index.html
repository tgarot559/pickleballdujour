Tip #1 · Singles

In singles, return deep and move up

In singles, you cover the whole court alone. A deep return gives you time to reach the net before the server's third shot.

Tip #2 · Serve

Serve deep, not hard

A serve landing near the baseline pushes the receiver back and buys you time. Depth matters more than pace.

Tip #3 · Return

After the return, get to the kitchen

The receiving team has an edge: it can move to the net right after the return. Don't waste it by staying back.

Tip #4 · Strategy

The third shot is the drop

The serving team is stuck at the back. A drop, a soft ball into the opponent's kitchen, lets it move up without being punished.

Tip #5 · Technique

Paddle up, always

At the net, exchanges are too fast to bring your paddle up from your waist. Keep it in front, at chest height.

Tip #6 · Strategy

Dinking is patience

A dink rally is rarely won by a brilliant shot. It's won when the other side loses patience and pops the ball up.

Tip #7 · Doubles

Three words that save a doubles game

"Mine", "yours", "leave it". In doubles, half the points lost down the middle come from silence.

Tip #8 · Rules

Watch your momentum after a volley

Volley just in front of the line, then step into the kitchen, even after the rally ends: that's a fault. Momentum counts.

Tip #9 · Strategy

Aim at the feet, not the head

A ball arriving at a net player's feet is almost impossible to return aggressively.

Tip #10 · Health

Five minutes of warm-up

Pickleball injuries mostly hit ankles, calves and shoulders. Five minutes are enough to prevent most of them.

Tip #11 · Rules

Call the score out loud

In doubles, the score has three numbers and everyone gets lost. Calling it before each serve prevents arguments.

Tip #12 · Technique

Dink to the backhand

For most players the backhand is weaker, especially at the net. Send your dinks there.

Tip #13 · Strategy

Lob, but rarely

A good lob is beautiful. A short lob is a gift-wrapped smash. Keep it as a surprise.

Tip #14 · Technique

Learn the reset

Under pressure, don't counterattack: slow down. A soft ball into the kitchen puts everyone back on equal terms.

Tip #15 · Gear

Use the right ball

Outdoor ball indoors: it bounces too much and skids. Indoor ball outdoors: the wind takes it.

Tip #16 · Technique

One grip for everything

The "hammer", or continental, grip lets you play forehand, backhand and volley without changing grip.

Tip #17 · Strategy

Don't stay mid-court

The area between the baseline and the kitchen, the transition zone, is where balls land at your feet. Move through it.

Tip #18 · Doubles

Move as if you're tied together

In doubles, picture a ten-foot rope between you and your partner. When one moves, the other follows.

Tip #19 · Strategy

Speed up the right ball

A speed-up at the wrong moment comes back faster than it left. Wait for a ball above the net.

Tip #20 · Outdoors

Play the wind

Wind behind you: play shorter and softer. Wind in your face: you can hit harder, the ball will be slowed.

Tip #21 · Mindset

One point at a time

Only the serving team scores: you can lose six rallies without losing a point. Don't panic.

Tip #22 · Advanced

The erne, for bold players

Going around the kitchen to volley from outside the court is legal, as long as you don't touch the zone.

Tip #23 · Gear

Beginner? Go light

A 7 to 8 oz paddle forgives mistakes and protects your elbow. You can move to heavier later.

Tip #24 · Doubles

When in doubt, go middle

A ball between the two opponents creates hesitation and cuts the angles they can send back.

Tip #25 · Health

Shoes before the paddle

Pickleball means lateral movement and sudden stops. Running shoes are a sprain waiting to happen.
