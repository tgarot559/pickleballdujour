Pickleball court dimensions

In shortA pickleball court is 44 ft long and 20 ft wide (13.41 × 6.10 m), for both singles and doubles. The kitchen extends 7 ft (2.13 m) on each side of the net. The net is 34 inches (86 cm) at the centre and 36 inches (91 cm) at the posts. With run-off space, allow at least 30 × 60 ft (about 9 × 18 m).

Lines belong to the area they mark: a ball touching the baseline is in, and the kitchen line is part of the kitchen.

Same size as a badminton court

A pickleball court is the size of a doubles badminton court, so many sports halls can host pickleball with tape lines. The net is lower than in badminton, closer to tennis height.

How many courts fit on a tennis court?

Four, according to the French Tennis Federation. That's why tennis clubs are adopting it so quickly: one quiet tennis court can host sixteen players at once.

Lay out a court yourself

  1. Mark the 44 × 20 ft rectangle. Check it's square by measuring both diagonals: they must be equal (about 48 ft 4 in, or 14.73 m).
  2. Find the middle of the length for the net, then mark both kitchen lines 7 ft either side.
  3. Mark the centreline on each side, from the kitchen line to the baseline. It doesn't cross the kitchen.
  4. Set up the net: 34 in at the centre, 36 in at the posts. A portable net is perfect for casual play.

Resin or porous concrete are the recommended surfaces; sports halls work too. Clay doesn't: the plastic ball won't bounce properly.

Frequently asked questions

Is the court the same for singles and doubles?

Yes, 44 × 20 ft (13.41 × 6.10 m) in both cases.

How big is the kitchen?

7 ft (2.13 m) deep on each side of the net, across the full width of the court.

Can you play on a badminton court?

Yes, it's the same size as a doubles badminton court. You just need a lower net and the kitchen lines.

How much space do you need to build a court?

At least 30 × 60 ft (about 9 × 18 m) for tournaments, including run-off. Casual courts can be a bit tighter, but keep room behind the baselines.
